In gauge or out of gauge?

In gauge: Cargo fits completely inside the walls and below the top edge (inside height about 2.35 m). The tarpaulin closes over it and it is priced like a normal open top.

Out of gauge (OOG): Cargo taller than the walls is 'out of gauge' (over-height). The tarpaulin cannot close, nothing can be stacked on top of it on the ship, and the line charges extra for the space it blocks. It needs the shipping line's approval before booking.

Send us the exact length, width, height and weight of each piece, ideally with a drawing, and we will tell you if it fits in gauge.

Floor and weightHeavy-duty floor built for concentrated weight, but load must still be spread and secured to the lashing points.
In gauge or out of gauge?

In gauge: Cargo stays within the outline of a normal container: no wider than the rack (2.44 m outside) and no taller than a standard container (2.59 m including the rack floor). It is handled almost like a normal box.

Out of gauge (OOG): Cargo that is wider (over-width), taller (over-height) or longer (over-length) than that outline is 'out of gauge'. It takes up space next to or above it on the ship, so the line charges for those lost slots, and it may need special road permits or escorts in India.

For out-of-gauge cargo we need exact dimensions, weight, centre of gravity and lifting points before the line will accept the booking. Ask us early.

THE WORDS, EXPLAINED

What the numbers mean.

Tare

The weight of the empty container itself.

Payload

The most cargo you can put inside, including pallets and packing.

Max gross

Container plus cargo. The total that must never be exceeded.

CBM (m³)

Cubic metres: length × width × height. How much space your cargo takes.

TEU / FEU

Twenty-foot (TEU) and forty-foot (FEU) equivalent units. A 40′ counts as 2 TEU.

High cube

A container 30 cm taller than standard: 2.90 m outside instead of 2.59 m.

In gauge

Cargo that fits within the normal outline of a container, so it ships like a regular box.

Out of gauge (OOG)

Cargo that sticks out over the top, sides or ends of an open top or flat rack. Needs approval, special stowage and costs more.

VGM

Verified Gross Mass: the declared total weight of a packed container before shipping.

Questions people ask

Can I really load 28 tonnes into a 20 foot container?+

The container can take it, but the road often cannot. Truck weight limits in India and in the destination country may cap what you can carry on the road leg. Tell us your route and we will tell you the safe maximum.

What is the difference between a 40′ and a 40′ high cube?+

Same length and width, but the high cube is 30 cm taller inside. That gives about 9 m³ more space. The price difference is usually small, so for light, bulky cargo the high cube is often the better deal.

How much cargo really fits in a container?+

Less than the full volume, because cartons never fit perfectly and you need space to load. Plan on about 25–28 m³ in a 20′, 55–58 m³ in a 40′ and 65–68 m³ in a 40′ high cube. Our container load calculator can work it out from your packing list.

Where can I see the exact weights of my container?+

On the CSC safety plate and the markings on the container door. They show the exact empty weight (tare) and maximum gross weight for that particular box, which can differ slightly from the typical figures here.

What is VGM?+

Verified Gross Mass: the total weight of the container including cargo, packing and the container itself. It must be declared to the shipping line before the container is loaded on the ship.

Which container should I choose?+

Heavy, dense cargo usually goes in a 20′. Light, bulky cargo goes in a 40′ or 40′ high cube. Temperature-sensitive goods need a reefer, and oversized or very heavy items need an open top or flat rack. WhatsApp us your packing list and we will suggest the best fit.
